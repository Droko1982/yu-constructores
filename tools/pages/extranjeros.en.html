Building a house in Colombia as a foreigner

We are YU Construcciones S.A.S., a construction company based in Armenia, Quindío (Colombia), the capital of the department, in Colombia's Coffee Region. More and more foreigners are choosing this region to live, retire or have a holiday home, and almost all of them arrive with the same questions. This guide brings them together.

It is general guidance written by a construction company, not legal advice. For the purchase of the plot and the foreign-exchange and tax aspects, work with an independent lawyer.

Can a foreigner buy a plot and build in Colombia?

Yes. Colombia's Constitution recognises the same civil rights for foreigners as for Colombians, and no visa is needed to buy property: a passport is enough. The property is registered in your name with the same guarantees as a Colombian national's.

The step most often skipped: registering the investment

If you do not reside in Colombia, buying a plot and building is foreign investment. The money must come in through the foreign-exchange market — a bank or an authorised intermediary — with its foreign-exchange declaration, and the investment is registered with the Banco de la República (Colombia's central bank). That is what gives you the right to take the money out when you sell. Skipping it is a foreign-exchange violation and can cost you that right. Do it with your bank and your lawyer from the very first transfer.

Before you buy the plot

  • Land use. The municipal land-use plan (POT, PBOT or EOT) states whether housing can be built on that property, with what minimum area and whether it has risk zones. A cheap plot may be cheap because it cannot be built on.
  • Title. The certificado de tradición y libertad (title certificate), issued by the Oficina de Registro de Instrumentos Públicos (land registry), shows the owner, mortgages, court attachments and easements. Your lawyer carries out the title search.
  • Rural properties. Some rural plots that were once public land allocated by the State are subject to size limits (Unidad Agrícola Familiar, the family farm unit). Your lawyer should check this before the preliminary purchase agreement (promesa de compraventa).
  • Utilities and access. Water, power, road and slope. In the countryside, water may depend on a rural community water system or on a water concession from the environmental authority, which in Quindío is the CRQ (regional environmental authority).
  • Coffee Cultural Landscape. The region has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 2011, and some rural areas carry additional requirements on what can be built.

Within Quindío we can visit the plot with you before you buy it, free of charge, and tell you what an engineer sees: slope, soil, retaining needs, access and utilities.

The building permit

New construction, extensions and structural strengthening require a building permit. In Armenia permits are issued by the curadurías urbanas (municipal building-permit offices); in the other municipalities, by the planning office. The application includes the architectural drawings, the structural design and, generally, the soil study, signed by professionals licensed to practise in Colombia. If your architect works outside the country, their designs can be used, but what is filed is signed by professionals with a Colombian professional registration.

The permit is issued in the owner's name. We support the process and coordinate with the designer and the issuing authority.

Building in an earthquake zone

Quindío is a high seismic hazard zone. The earthquake of 25 January 1999 destroyed much of Armenia, and the earthquake of 10 August 2026 damaged buildings across the region once again. That is why all construction is governed by Colombia's earthquake-resistant building code, the NSR-10: calculated structural design, quality-controlled materials and supervised execution. What protects your investment most is not the finish; it is the structure you cannot see.

Climate, terrain and timing

The climate is temperate highland, and the region has two rainy seasons a year. Almost all plots are on hillsides, so earthworks, retaining structures and drainage are a central part of the budget. We schedule those stages so they are not left exposed in the middle of the rainy season.

Costs to plan for on top of the construction

  • Notary fees, registration fees and registration tax when the deed to the plot is executed.
  • Fees for the design, the technical studies and the permit.
  • Property tax, paid every year to the municipality.
  • Utility connections or, in the countryside, a septic system and a water intake.

Visas and residence

Buying property does not in itself grant residence. An investment of a certain amount can serve as the basis for an investor visa, but the requirements and amounts are set by the Cancillería (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) and change over time: check them there or with an immigration lawyer.

Language and documents

The deed, the permit and the contracts are executed in Spanish. If you are not fluent, the notary's office requires an interpreter to be present at signing; do not leave it until the last day. Throughout the project we reply in writing in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French or German. Calls and video calls are in Spanish, or in English when booked in advance, so that our English-speaking consultant can take part.

Why Quindío

It lies at the heart of the Coffee Region, between Pereira, Manizales and northern Valle del Cauca. Armenia has El Edén International Airport with daily flights to Bogotá, and the nearby towns — Salento, Filandia, Circasia, Montenegro — account for much of the region's country housing.
