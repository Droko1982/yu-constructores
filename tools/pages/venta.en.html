Why a country house in Quindío

Quindío is the smallest department in the Coffee Region and one of the most sought-after places to live in the countryside without moving far from the city. It has a temperate mountain climate and a landscape of coffee and guadua (a native bamboo), and much of its rural area forms part of the Coffee Cultural Landscape (Paisaje Cultural Cafetero), inscribed by UNESCO in 2011. Armenia, the capital, has El Edén International Airport, and Pereira's Matecaña airport is about an hour away by road. That is why the people who buy houses here include families from Bogotá, Medellín or Cali as well as Colombians living abroad and foreigners looking for a place to retire.

What to check before buying a country house

We are builders, not just sellers, so we will tell you what we would check ourselves. This is general guidance: for the legal side, rely on a lawyer.

  • The legal status. The land registry certificate (certificado de tradición y libertad) shows who the owner is and whether the property has mortgages, court attachments or easements. Your lawyer should review it before the preliminary purchase agreement.
  • Land use and the permit for what has been built. Ask whether the house has a building permit or a reconocimiento (legal recognition of an existing building): a house built without a permit can complicate financing and any future sale. We explain this in the building permit guide.
  • The structure. In a high seismic hazard zone such as Quindío, it matters how the house was built and to which code. See what the NSR-10 requires.
  • The land and the water. On a hillside, the way rainwater is handled, the slopes and the walls say a great deal about how the house will age. Also ask where the drinking water comes from and how wastewater is treated.
  • Access and utilities. The condition of the road in the rainy season, electricity, gas and the internet connection.
  • The estate. The amount of the service charge, what it covers, and the estate's bylaws, which may lay down rules on extensions, pets or letting.

If you are buying from outside Colombia

A foreigner can buy a home in Colombia with the same rights as a Colombian national. Read the guide for foreigners and, if you live abroad, how the paperwork is signed remotely with a power of attorney, in building in Colombia from abroad.
